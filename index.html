About

Research grounded in field experience and conservation delivery.

I am a Senior Forestry Officer with the Nature Conservation Division, Department of Forests and Park Services, Bhutan. My research focuses on wildlife ecology, conservation science and human–wildlife coexistence, with particular interests in the movement behaviour, population dynamics and ecology of wild cats and their prey.

My broader work spans camera trapping, GPS collaring, advanced ecological analyses, wildlife statistics, research design, community perception studies and the movement ecology of riverine fishes. I also contribute as a trainer in ecological research methods.

OngoingNationwide · Bhutan

Nationwide Musk Deer & Takin Survey

Implemented with support from the Bhutan for Life Project and WWF's programme on reducing the extirpation of musk deer in Bhutan, this nationwide initiative combines species conservation with community livelihood considerations.

It is Bhutan's first large-scale monitoring programme for wild ungulates using extensive arrays of camera traps together with modern statistical approaches. The survey is designed to generate comparable information on distribution, occurrence and population status across important musk deer and takin landscapes.

The evidence generated will support long-term conservation planning, improve future monitoring design and help identify priority areas where species protection and community-based conservation actions can be strengthened together.

Camera trappingWild ungulatesPopulation monitoringCommunity conservation
Partners & project focus

Generate robust information on musk deer and takin while strengthening long-term conservation planning and supporting approaches that also protect and improve the livelihoods of communities sharing these landscapes.

OngoingRiver systems · Bhutan

Golden Mahseer Movement & Spawning Ecology

Golden mahseer is a large riverine carp whose habitat is increasingly affected by changes in river systems. This project investigates movement ecology and spawning behaviour of golden mahseer together with the related chocolate mahseer.

The work follows how fish move through river networks, identifies important migratory and spawning areas, and examines threats encountered along those routes. Particular attention is given to habitat connectivity and illegal fishing pressure.

By linking movement information with threats along migratory pathways, the project aims to identify river reaches and habitats that require priority conservation and management.

Project focus

Understand migratory pathways, spawning behaviour, threats along migration routes and illegal fishing pressure to guide riverine habitat conservation.

Baseline studyRoyal Manas National Park

Pangolin Distribution, Genetics & Community Knowledge

This Royal Manas National Park project is Bhutan's first systematic study to bring together pangolin distribution, community knowledge and genetic information within one research programme.

Earlier pangolin studies in Bhutan were largely localized. The current work establishes a stronger baseline by combining field evidence with information from communities and genetic samples to improve understanding of pangolin status and species composition.

The project is intended to provide a foundation for a future nationwide assessment and to contribute evidence toward development of Bhutan's first national pangolin conservation action plan.

PangolinDistributionGeneticsCommunity knowledge
Project focus

Build the baseline evidence needed for nationwide assessment of pangolin status, species composition and conservation priorities in Bhutan.

Scaling nationallyBhutan

Environmental DNA for Biodiversity Monitoring

Bhutan is expanding environmental DNA (eDNA) as an efficient and comparatively cost-effective approach for biodiversity monitoring, building on an earlier pilot study supported by WWF Bhutan.

The current work is scaling eDNA toward nationwide biodiversity assessment. The Department of Forests and Park Services has also embedded the approach within the WildinSync programme with WWF, with technical support from ETH Zürich and the College of Natural Resources.

Beyond generating biodiversity information, the programme is building national technical capacity in field sampling, laboratory workflows, data interpretation and long-term monitoring, with the wider ambition of strengthening Bhutan's role as a regional hub for eDNA technology.

eDNAMetabarcodingNational baselineCapacity building
Partners & project focus

Use eDNA to strengthen national biodiversity monitoring while developing Bhutanese technical capacity for long-term application of the technology.

Partners/support: DoFPS · Bhutan Trust Fund for Environmental Conservation · WWF · ETH Zürich · College of Natural Resources

First-of-its-kind programmeBhutan

Bhutan Wild Cat Health Project

This first-of-its-kind programme examines wild cat health and interactions with domestic dogs across a broad elevational gradient, from temperate forest to alpine mountain scrub.

A network of approximately 400 camera traps is being used to document wild cats, domestic dogs and their spatial interactions. The project also incorporates non-invasive scat sampling to investigate disease prevalence in Bhutan's wild felids.

Specially trained dogs are used to help locate wild-cat scat, combining camera-based ecological information with biological samples to strengthen understanding of wildlife health and potential disease interfaces.

Partners & project focus

Research studyBhutan

Golden Langur Highway Study

This study examines how roads and associated habitat conditions influence golden langur occurrence and abundance along highway landscapes in Bhutan.

Repeated field observations are analysed together with forest and road-related covariates to distinguish habitat effects from infrastructure effects. The study also evaluates where road exposure may overlap with important golden langur habitat.

The aim is to translate field evidence into practical recommendations for road planning, mitigation and long-term conservation of golden langurs in increasingly modified landscapes.

Project focus

Link repeated field observations with habitat and road covariates to understand how transport infrastructure intersects with golden langur conservation and where mitigation may be most useful.
