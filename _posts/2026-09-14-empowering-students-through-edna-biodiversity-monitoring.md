Students at **Tshaphel Middle Secondary School in Haa** are gaining hands-on experience with **environmental DNA (eDNA)**, a modern biodiversity monitoring tool that helps detect species from genetic traces left in water, soil, and other environments. The initiative brings conservation science into the classroom and the field, helping young learners connect Bhutan’s rich biodiversity with practical skills for protecting it.

The **Nature Conservation Division**, under the **Department of Forests and Park Services**, in partnership with the **Bhutan Trust Fund for Environmental Conservation (BTFEC)**, **World Wildlife Fund (WWF)**, and **ETH Zurich**, is mainstreaming eDNA as a practical tool for biodiversity monitoring across Bhutan.

Environmental DNA refers to genetic material that organisms leave behind in their surroundings, such as water, soil, or sediment. By collecting and analyzing these traces, conservation teams can detect the presence of species more efficiently and strengthen biodiversity monitoring in protected areas, landscapes managed by Divisional Forest Offices, and Bhutan’s biological corridors.

## Taking Conservation Science to Schools

Beyond its use in national biodiversity monitoring, the Department of Forests and Park Services is introducing eDNA to students to inspire curiosity, scientific learning, and environmental responsibility among Bhutan’s **future guardians of natural heritage**.

As part of this initiative, students of **Tshaphel Middle Secondary School in Haa** are taking part in a **student-led eDNA biodiversity monitoring program** in their local environment.

Through hands-on field sampling and practical learning activities, students explore modern biodiversity monitoring techniques and build a deeper understanding of the ecosystems and species found around them.

## Building the Next Generation of Conservationists

![Students carrying out eDNA sample collection during a field activity](/assets/news/edna-tshaphel-school/students-edna-sampling.jpg)
*Students try out eDNA sample collection during a field-based learning activity.*

The program aims to strengthen students’ knowledge of:

- Biodiversity and ecosystem conservation
- Environmental DNA and modern conservation science
- Field-based biodiversity monitoring
- Environmental stewardship and responsible use of natural resources
- Citizen science and community participation in conservation

By involving students directly in biodiversity monitoring, the initiative bridges classroom learning and real-world conservation practice. It encourages young people to observe, understand, and protect Bhutan’s natural resources through science-based action.

The student-led eDNA program shows how **science, education, and conservation can come together** to nurture informed young conservationists and strengthen Bhutan’s long-term commitment to protecting its exceptional biodiversity.
