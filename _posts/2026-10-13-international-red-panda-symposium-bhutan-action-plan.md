From **5 to 9 October 2026**, I had the opportunity to participate in the **International Red Panda Symposium 2026**, organized by the **Red Panda Network** in **Kathmandu, Nepal**. The symposium provided a valuable platform to exchange conservation experiences, share ideas and explore opportunities for regional and international collaboration.

My presentation focused primarily on **Bhutan's Red Panda Conservation Action Plan (2025–2035)**, outlining the country's key conservation priorities for this threatened Himalayan species. Beyond presenting Bhutan's approach, the event offered opportunities to exchange ideas with researchers, conservation practitioners and other participants on future partnerships.

## A Decade-Long Vision for Red Panda Conservation

The action plan recognises that effective red panda conservation requires more than protecting individual animals. It calls for an integrated approach combining habitat management, ecological research, threat reduction and stronger participation by communities living alongside the species.

**Habitat protection and management** is a central priority. Red pandas are particularly sensitive to habitat disturbance, making the maintenance of suitable forest habitat, ecological connectivity and efforts to minimise human-induced pressures essential for their long-term persistence.

The plan also prioritises **research into red panda ecology and ecological relationships**. Strengthening knowledge of habitat requirements, distribution, behaviour and associations within the wider forest ecosystem will support more targeted and adaptive management decisions.

A further priority is **reducing disease-related and other threats**. Better understanding of wildlife health risks, stronger surveillance and preventive conservation measures are important elements of a comprehensive species conservation strategy.

## Linking Red Panda Conservation with Community Livelihoods

An important message of my presentation was the need to **enhance coexistence by ensuring conservation also creates opportunities for communities sharing red panda habitat**.

I highlighted the potential of **red panda-focused wildlife tourism** as a means of generating conservation-compatible livelihoods through activities such as local guiding, community-based accommodation and related services. Such opportunities could strengthen local incentives for habitat stewardship.

At the same time, wildlife tourism must be carefully planned. Given the species' sensitivity to disturbance, any tourism initiative should be informed by scientific evidence, visitor management standards, habitat safeguards and meaningful involvement of local communities. The objective is to make **red panda conservation and the well-being of cohabiting communities mutually reinforcing**.

## Exchanges and Opportunities for Collaboration

The symposium was also an opportunity to exchange ideas and identify possible areas for cooperation in ecological research, habitat management, wildlife health, community participation and sustainable conservation approaches.

For a species associated with Himalayan ecosystems, sharing experience across conservation programmes and countries can help strengthen the evidence and practical partnerships needed for its long-term protection.

## Moving from Planning to Action

As Bhutan advances its **Red Panda Conservation Action Plan (2025–2035)**, implementing its priorities will require continued scientific monitoring, institutional coordination, field-based conservation and support from local communities.

I returned from Kathmandu encouraged by the opportunities for professional exchange and potential collaboration. The symposium reinforced an important principle: **long-term conservation success depends on protecting suitable habitats, deepening ecological understanding, reducing threats and ensuring people living alongside wildlife have a stake in its future**.
